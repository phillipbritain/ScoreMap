# MapLibre GL for the globe

The globe is rendered with MapLibre GL's globe projection over vector map tiles, not a three.js textured sphere such as globe.gl. A realistic textured Earth looks more striking, but it blurs when zoomed in and has no built-in borders, place labels or clustering. ScoreMap needs crowded areas to stay readable and needs clear borders and city names for its geography goal. A satellite style can be added later as an alternative map style.

Revisited in October 2026, when the default light map style didn't feel right. A globe.gl prototype (branch `prototype-globe-gl`) tried a photographic day Earth, a night-lights Earth and a plain dark globe with border lines. The dark globe won on looks, but in globe.gl the photographic Earths blurred close in, place names would need hand-built overlap handling, and so would clustering. So the globe stays on MapLibre and takes that look instead: the tiles' data drawn as a plain navy globe with thin light coastlines, borders and state lines, grey place names, a starry background and an even blue glow. MapLibre's own atmosphere is lit from one side and can't be recoloured, so the glow is a circle drawn behind the map.
