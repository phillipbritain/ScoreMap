# Games with a field or a fight card

ScoreMap was built for games between two sides, but motor racing and golf are contested by a field, and a UFC event is a card of bouts (#220). We keep them as **games**, rather than a new kind of thing beside games, so pins, score cards, clusters, the game panel, statuses and polling stay one path: a game's score takes one of three shapes, two **sides**, a **field** of **entrants**, or a **fight card** of **bouts**. `ProviderGame` on the server and `Game` in the browser (ADR-0003) replace `Home` and `Away` with a contest that is one of `Sides { Home, Away }`, `Field { Entries }` or `Bouts { Bouts }`, a tagged union in the browser, so the type checker finds every place that assumed two sides. We weighed instead leaving `Home` and `Away` nullable beside optional `Field` and `Bouts`, which every reader would have to remember to check, and reusing `Home` and `Away` for 1st and 2nd, which would quietly break anything about home cities and teams.

What one game is follows what counts as a result. An F1 weekend is one game, its race: practice doesn't count and qualifying only sets the grid, so ESPN's FP1–FP3 and Qualifying competitions are left out, and NASCAR and F1 are alike one game per event. A golf tournament is one game, Live for all four days, its nights between rounds being breaks as halftime is, rather than one game a round (no one follows a round on its own) or a new between-rounds status. A UFC card is one game, not one a bout: ESPN gives every bout the card's start time, so bouts as games would all go Live at once and sit Live for hours, twelve pins on one venue.

Checked against ESPN responses saved in `server/tests/ScoreMap.Server.Tests/Fixtures/Espn/` in October 2026: NASCAR at Talladega (`nascar-final.json`) and Martinsville before it started (`nascar.json`), the Mexico City Grand Prix during FP2 (`f1.json`), the Mexico Open during round 2 (`golf-pga.json`), a UFC Fight Night (`ufc.json`), and the core API's NASCAR event and venue and golf event (`*-core-*.json`).

## Consequences

- A field game's score card keeps today's size, two rows and a progress line: the top two entrants by position ("1 K. Larson"), with golf's score to par beside them and no score in racing, where the feed gives positions but no gaps. The progress line gives the lap, or golf's round and holes ("R2 · Thru 14", "After R2"). A fight card's score card shows the bout in progress as two sides, else the main event. Card layout is unchanged.
- A field game's game panel shows the top 10 with "Show all" for the rest of the field; a fight card's shows every bout, main event first, with its result and method.
- A field game pulses when the lead changes hands (a tie for the lead isn't a change until someone leads alone), a fight card when a bout finishes, and both when they start and finish.
- A golf game is hot in the final round once the leader has played 9 holes or more, leading by no more than 1 shot; a fight card while its main event is in its last scheduled round. A race is never hot: ESPN gives neither its total laps nor the gaps between cars, and would have to before a rule could tell a close finish.
- NASCAR and golf scoreboards give no venue. The server takes a field game's venue from its scoreboard when there is one (F1's `circuit`, UFC's `venue`), else once per event from ESPN's core API (`sports.core.api.espn.com`, an event's `venues` or golf's `courses`), saved like other lookups, and the venue locator places it as any other. The core API is a second unofficial ESPN endpoint, under ADR-0001's terms.
- A field game's or fight card's id is ESPN's event id, so Top Events matching by event id is unchanged.
- A golf game's `PlannedLength` (ADR-0005) is about four days.
